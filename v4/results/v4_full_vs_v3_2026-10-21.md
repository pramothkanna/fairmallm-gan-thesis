RQ3 trade-off in v4.3: D2 feedback + label parity lowers gender leakage from 0.72 to 0.60
(v3: 0.63 → 0.58) at a cost of 0.017 AUC (v3: 0.008). With the more realistic v4 data the
fairness component removes clearly more gender information than in v3.

Caution: the GRU prediction fairness does NOT follow the data fairness here — the
ablation's GRU has smaller SPD/EOD (−0.063/−0.029) than the proposed model's
(−0.121/−0.071). The two runs also drew different real same-n reference samples (AUC 0.761
vs 0.742, SPD +0.016 vs −0.092), i.e. per-run numbers are noisy. The joint 5-seed
evaluation (`fairmallm-evaluate-joint`, same test students and seeds for all sets) decides.

What this settles
1. **RQ2 / realism:** v4.3 roughly halves the temporal distribution error vs v3
   (W1 0.145 vs 0.331, lag-1 gap 0.18 vs 0.35), D1 0.68 vs 0.83, and lifts TSTR AUC from
   0.686 to 0.721 (proposed) / 0.737 (no D2), at or above CTGAN (0.724). The GRU
   discriminative score is unchanged (≈0.37): a sequence classifier still separates synthetic
   from real students (click curves too smooth, see weekly_curves.png).
2. **RQ1 / fairness of the data:** with D2, gender leakage 0.60 vs 0.72 without D2 (real 0.76);
   CTGAN amplifies it (0.86).
3. **RQ3 trade-off:** D2 costs 0.016 AUC (0.737 → 0.721) for 0.11 less leakage.
4. **The prediction-fairness gap is real, not noise.** On the same test set the GRU trained on
   the proposed model's data has SPD −0.115 ± 0.013 vs −0.070 ± 0.008 without D2 (EOD −0.060
   vs −0.035). Removing gender information from the training data does not make the
   predictions on REAL students fairer; here it makes them less fair. A plausible explanation
   (to be tested, e.g. with the within-module analysis already in results/analysis/C_*):
   real test students still differ by gender in engagement and module mix; a model trained on
   gender-blind synthetic data applies one engagement→risk mapping to everybody and so flags
   the group with lower typical engagement in its modules (here women) more often, while
   data that keep the real gender–feature structure let the model account for it.
   Combining real and synthetic data gives near-zero SPD for every LLM variant.

# Addendum 22 Oct 2026 — analysis v4 vs v3 (`results/analysis_v4/` in the repository)

* **Within-module fairness settles point 4 above.** Size-weighted GRU SPD inside the modules is +0.070 for
  both v4.3 models (v3 +0.073/+0.074, real +0.047/+0.050): within modules every model flags men slightly more.
  The overall difference (−0.115 vs −0.070) lies entirely between modules (−0.185 vs −0.140), i.e. it is the
  module-composition proxy (65 % of women study in the low-click modules BBB and GGG). Why D2 increases the
  between-module part is not shown yet (hypothesis: gender-blind data remove the low-click-but-passing
  students in BBB/GGG, so the GRU learns "few clicks = at risk" more strictly).
* **Value sizes now realistic:** mean quiz value in active weeks 42.4 (real 41.0, v3 21.9); scores 66.9 (real 76.9).
* **Label signal:** quiz-week gap pass vs at-risk 8.5 pp (real 8.6, v3 5.6); click gap still exaggerated
  (1.74 vs 1.02 real); logistic regression AUC on real test 0.748 / 0.757 (v3 0.687 / 0.705, CTGAN 0.762).
* **Regression:** feature-correlation gap 0.87 for both v4.3 sets (v3 0.39 / 0.33, CTGAN 0.79): clicks are no
  longer coupled to the planned quiz/assessment weeks — next thing to fix.
